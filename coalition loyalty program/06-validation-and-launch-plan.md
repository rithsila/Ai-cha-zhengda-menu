# Validation and launch plan

Illustrative 90-day sequence. It is an experiment schedule, not a promise that legal review or software development can finish in 90 days. No outreach has been sent.

## Days 1–14: learn before committing to a rebuild

Founder chooses one reachable Phnom Penh neighborhood, ideally with coffee, lunch, snacks, and dinner options. Interview 15 owners/managers and 30 potential customers. Include cash users and people who already use bank rewards. Do not recruit only friends or customers who love prizes.

Record each merchant's category, location, average ticket, estimated paid orders, existing promotions/software cost, busy-hour workflow, margin range if voluntarily shared, preferred payment methods, and decision maker. Use estimates as estimates; seek anonymized sales totals to improve the model.

### Merchant interview

1. How do you currently encourage repeat visits, and what does it cost?
2. What menu/POS tools do you pay for, and what causes daily trouble?
3. What evidence would make you believe a reward scheme brings profitable extra business?
4. How would you feel about your customers earning here and redeeming at another shop?
5. For every $100 eligible sales, would $2 reward funding plus $0.20 service fee be acceptable? Why or why not?
6. Would you pay $15/month for this menu/workflow package? Compare their current alternative, not just yes/no.
7. Which items could you offer at $1 or $2 reimbursement? What stock/time restrictions are necessary?
8. Can staff verify a paid receipt without slowing the queue? Demonstrate on a realistic order.
9. What payout interval, prefunding amount, and refund process could you accept?
10. Would you sign a conditional pilot agreement stating the actual costs and start requirements?

### Customer interview

Ask about the last three café/restaurant visits, existing rewards use, typical nearby shops, willingness to create an account, and whether the rewards are understandable. Show: “Spend $50 across these shops to earn a $1 reward.” Ask them to explain it back and choose between that offer, immediate cashback, and a shop stamp card.

Then test Gold and a hypothetical $50 top-up separately: why prepay, what value is expected, what happens if the favorite shop leaves, and would they still use the service without a phone giveaway? Record objections, not just interest.

### Discovery decision

Target at least five independent merchants willing to sign conditional pilot terms and fund rewards, with a credible customer overlap. If fewer commit, revise the price, workflow, or neighborhood before building the coalition. Do not treat free registrations as willingness to pay.

## Days 15–30: agreements, partner review, and prototype validation

Obtain written local legal/accounting advice on report 05's questions. Seek proposals from suitable banks/payment providers using their official business channels. Compare all fees, custody permissions, event verification, payout support, reversals, KYC, and onboarding time. These are founder-led future actions, not completed contacts.

Test a clickable or paper earning/redemption flow with five cashiers and ten customers. Measure time and mistakes. Define MVP requirements using real feedback; use report 07 as the starting gap list. Establish a two-week shop sales/repeat-visit baseline where practical.

Select 5–10 shops. Proposed composition: at least two cafés and several complementary meal/snack shops within a compact area. Product eligibility must follow legal review. Have each shop publish a small, deliverable reward catalog and a capped funding budget.

## Days 31–60: prepare and run a controlled pilot when gates pass

Build or adapt only the needed tenant, transaction, ledger, funding, and reporting flows. Test isolation, duplicate confirmations, concurrent redemption, refunds, merchant exit, and reconciliation. Train each shop with real devices and an ordinary purchase/refund demonstration.

Launch invitation-only with a proposed 200–500 registered customers across the cohort. This is a recruitment target, not an active-user forecast. Earned rewards first; no consumer top-up or lucky draw by default. If readiness is late, move the pilot dates rather than bypassing launch gates.

Review transactions and merchant balances daily. Review shop outcomes weekly. Keep enough coverage for every promised reward regardless of actual redemption speed.

## Days 61–90: evaluate and decide

Proposed decision thresholds below are management hypotheses, not industry benchmarks. With a small sample, results are directional. Define them before launch so success is not redefined afterward.

| Metric | Definition | Proposed pilot threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Merchant activation | Trained shops recording a valid earn and redemption / signed shops | At least 80% |
| Merchant paid continuation | Activated shops agreeing to continue at disclosed full price/funding | At least 70% |
| Customer activation | Registered users with a first verified earn within 7 days | At least 40% |
| 30-day repeat | Activated customers with another eligible purchase within 30 days, among cohorts observed for 30 days | At least 25% |
| Cross-shop usage | Activated customers with eligible activity at 2+ merchants within 30 days | At least 15% |
| First reward attainment | Median days to first redemption among redeemers, also report percentage never redeeming | Aim for 30 days or less; diagnose low attainment |
| Redemption reliability | Successful valid redemption attempts / valid attempts | At least 99%; investigate every balance error |
| Settlement timeliness | Undisputed payouts completed within promised interval | 100% |
| Funds coverage | Available committed funds / non-duplicated outstanding obligations | At least 100%, plus agreed buffer |
| Operational load | Median/p95 cashier extra time and weekly support minutes per shop | Median at most 15 seconds; support fits actual margin |
| Merchant economics | Incremental contribution estimate minus rewards, fees and software | Positive directional result for a majority; disclose measurement uncertainty |

Do not compare members with nonmembers and claim causation: frequent customers may self-select. Where feasible, use staggered rollout or randomized invitations, observe a baseline, and compare matched periods accounting for holidays and promotions. Track both network-wide growth and shop-to-shop redistribution.

Pause expansion for unexplained money discrepancies, repeated redemption failures, unapproved regulated flows, or unmanageable fraud. If merchants will not renew at sustainable costs, retain the useful SaaS and reconsider the coalition.

Full launch funding cannot be estimated responsibly until scope and vendor quotes are available. Budget no phone prize for the discovery pilot.

## Draft merchant invitation — for founder use

“We are testing a digital menu and shared neighborhood rewards service for independent shops. Customers earn rewards on verified purchases and can redeem them at participating shops. You control your reward budget and items, and receive the agreed payment for valid redemptions. We would like to understand your current process before asking you to join. Could we arrange a 20-minute conversation? The proposed paid offer and reward costs will be shown clearly before any commitment.”

This is an interview invitation, not a revenue guarantee or an approved public advertisement. Obtain a Khmer version reviewed by a fluent local speaker before use.

## Weekly founder scorecard

Track mapped prospects, owner interviews, conditional agreements, activated shops, eligible sales, issued reward exposure, fees collected, unpaid settlements, funding coverage, active customer cohorts, cross-shop usage, refunds/fraud, support hours, and cash runway. Review whether each proposed feature solves a measured problem before adding it.
