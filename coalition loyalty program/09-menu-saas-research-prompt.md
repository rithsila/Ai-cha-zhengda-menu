# Prompt: research a Cambodia-first digital menu SaaS

Copy the prompt below into a new research session in this repository.

---

Help me research and plan how to turn my existing Ai-Cha & Zhengda menu project into a multi-tenant digital menu SaaS for independent cafés, tea shops, and small restaurants in Cambodia. I am a solo developer with limited resources. I need a realistic business and product plan, not implementation yet.

Read the existing repository and the documents in `coalition loyalty program/` first. The previous shared-coin concept is background only. Our new direction is a simpler software subscription business.

Scope:
- Each merchant has isolated menus, branding, staff, orders, settings, and reports. A merchant may have multiple branches.
- Customers pay the shop directly through cash or its existing payment provider. Do not assume our platform needs to hold customer funds.
- Optional loyalty points can only be earned and redeemed within the same merchant business. The merchant funds and fulfills its own rewards.
- No cross-shop coins, customer top-up wallet, merchant settlement network, cash-out, or prize draws in the MVP.
- Research whether digital menus alone are enough, or whether lightweight ordering and staff workflow are needed to sell the product.

Please complete the following:
1. Inspect the current app and distinguish existing working capabilities, source-code observations, and missing SaaS foundations. Check actual payment code rather than relying only on the README. Do not modify application code.
2. Research current Cambodian competitors, including POS systems, QR menus, ordering tools, and per-shop loyalty. Use official sources where possible, cite URLs and dates, and distinguish advertised features from verified usage. Compare actual published prices without inventing missing fees.
3. Identify the best initial merchant segment, its daily problems, and why it would pay instead of using Facebook, Telegram, paper menus, or an existing POS.
4. Produce a nine-block business model canvas and a clear value proposition. Assess software-only, software plus ordering, and software plus local loyalty options; recommend the smallest useful starting offer.
5. Define MVP, later features, and explicit exclusions. Consider Khmer/English, KHR/USD display, QR access, browser versus Telegram entry, mobile staff devices, unreliable connections, onboarding, and support. Treat requirements as hypotheses until evidence supports them.
6. Compare practical tenant/branch isolation and deployment approaches for the existing stack. Recommend a maintainable solo-developer approach and explain data boundaries, authorization, backups, migration, billing, and operating costs without overengineering.
7. Propose pricing experiments, transparent unit economics, monthly operating costs, merchant acquisition/support costs, and break-even scenarios. Clearly label every assumption and avoid treating projections as evidence.
8. Propose a 90-day validation plan with merchant/customer interview questions, a small pilot, measurable go/no-go criteria, and a prioritized roadmap. Separate discovery, engineering readiness, and launch requirements.
9. Identify proportionate Cambodian business, consumer, privacy, payment-integration, and tax questions requiring professional confirmation. Do not assume avoiding a wallet removes every obligation, and do not introduce regulated financial features unnecessarily.
10. End with a candid recommendation: what I should build first, what I should postpone, what could make this fail, and the first five actions I can take this week.

Save a readable research pack in `menu SaaS research/` with an overview, business canvas, competitor comparison, MVP/architecture options, economics, validation plan, and dated source register. Use plain English and explain unfamiliar terms. Separate verified facts, inferences, assumptions, and unanswered questions.

Proceed with reasonable assumptions and research autonomously. Ask only questions that materially affect the recommendation. Do not contact anyone, spend money, change application code, or deploy anything. This task is research and planning only.
