## Implications for your offer — inferences

1. **Do not compete on menu features alone.** There are free and low-priced alternatives in the inspected set.
2. **Compare total work, not just subscription.** If a cashier must copy every order to an existing POS, the extra workload can erase the benefit.
3. **Sell a bounded service.** Initial menu entry and staff training are tangible; unlimited ongoing manual edits are not sustainable at the proposed price.
4. **Keep loyalty optional.** Competitors already advertise it, and its value must exceed identity and redemption friction.
5. **Treat market claims cautiously.** A polished website, testimonial, “hundreds of businesses,” or instant-order mockup does not prove working deployments.

## Competitor demonstration checklist

1. Build one drink with size, sugar, ice and a paid topping; switch it to sold out.
2. Scan on a cheap Android phone in browser mode; place an order and recover after a connection interruption.
3. Check staff permissions, kitchen acknowledgement, cancellation and payment confirmation.
4. Ask for the full 12-month cost for one location, three staff and existing phones, including tax/setup/hardware/payment charges.
5. Ask how to export data, cancel, add a branch, and prevent one business accessing another's customer history.
