# Cambodia market and competitors

Desk research as of 9 September 2026. Official product pages establish advertised features, not independently verified active usage, merchant satisfaction, or contract economics.

## Market context

The NBC's June 2026 Cambodia–India QR announcement refers to 4.5 million KHQR merchant locations nationwide. This supports payment infrastructure readiness; it is **not** a count of unique independent restaurants, potential customers for this SaaS, or an addressable market estimate. [NBC announcement](https://www.nbc.gov.kh/english/news_and_events/news_info.php?id=908)

Existing reward programs show that the concept is familiar locally. They do not prove that customers want another app or that merchants will fund another scheme on top of current promotions.

## Direct rewards competition

| Program | Verified public offering | Competitive implication / evidence limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Wingpoints — Wing Bank** | Earn through participating WingPay/Wing KHQR purchases and redeem at participating outlets; published 12-month validity. [Official page](https://www.wingbank.com.kh/en/wingpoints) | Close substitute for the core cross-shop idea. Bank distribution and payment connection are advantages. Merchant fees, redemption reimbursement, and active network density were not verified. |
| **Sathapana Rewards** | Earn Stars and redeem across participating merchants through Sathapana Mobile. Page advertises 400+ partners, 12-month validity, and no cash conversion. Its conditions specify Sathapana QR earning, despite broader QR/KHQR wording elsewhere on the page. [Official page](https://www.sathapana.com.kh/en/digital-banking/personal/sathapana-rewards) | Another close substitute. Confirm exact payment eligibility and current nearby outlet coverage in a live demonstration. Published partner count is a provider claim, not an audited active count. |
| **ABA Cashback** | Free enrollment for eligible verified ABA Merchant businesses. Published conditions include at least 2% cashback, a minimum three-month offer, and the same percentage across branches. [Official page](https://www.ababank.com/en/aba-cashback/) | Strong alternative for merchant marketing budgets. Cash is easy to understand; a coin program must justify redemption friction. This is cashback, not evidence of a transferable coalition coin. |
| **SmartVIP — Smart Axiata** | Public page says $6+ monthly top-up unlocks VIP partner deals; qualifying postpaid/home-internet customers also qualify. Includes SmartVIP points and partner discounts. [Official page](https://www.smart.com.kh/zh/loyalty-programs) | Local precedent for top-up-linked VIP access. This is telecom qualification, not proof that selling multi-shop stored value has the same legal treatment. The linked localized page served English body text. |

## Shop software, chain loyalty, and discovery substitutes

| Product | Verified public offering | Implication / what remains unknown |
|---|---|---|
| **Point hub** | Cambodian POS with QR menus, orders, customer memberships, and loyalty app. Advertised annual plans: $99, $159, $259. [Official product/pricing page](https://pointhub.io/) | Direct software competitor. A $15/month menu product costs $180/year, so menu software alone may struggle against broader tools. Cross-merchant fungibility and settlement were not established. |
| **BROWN Reward** | Publishes $1 spent = 1 point, redemption for its products, virtual/physical cards, and top-ups. Membership-level page exists, but its image-based tier thresholds were not verified. [Program](https://www.browncoffee.com.kh/page/Brown_Card/), [levels](https://www.browncoffee.com.kh/page/Brown_Card/2) | Local customer benchmark for rewards and prepaid balances. It operates within a chain; do not infer unrelated-merchant acceptance. |
| **Starbucks Cambodia** | Official Cambodia app listing describes in-store payment, Stars, rewards, card balances, and order-ahead. [Developer listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_US&id=com.starbucks.kh) | Customer experience benchmark. US Gold/Reserve rules should not be assumed to apply in Cambodia. |
| **GoLoca** | Advertises Cambodian local discovery, menus, reservations, and digital loyalty stamps. [Product page](https://goloca.travel/en) | Adjacent competitor for discovery and shop relationships. Active scale and pooled cross-shop points were not verified; marketing site alone is insufficient. |
| **NomNote** | Phnom Penh app listing advertises discovery, reservations, events, and venue loyalty stamps. [Developer listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nomnote-discover-phnom-penh/id6761742960) | Watchlist competitor. Listed venue inventory does not establish loyalty-active partners or customer traction. |
| **TINGTOV** | Advertises merchant POS, inventory, loyalty, and a shared marketplace with a 1,000-riel completed-sale fee. [Official site](https://www.tingtov.com/) | Adjacent software/marketplace model. Verify operational availability, contract terms, and whether points transfer between merchants. |

Paper stamp cards, a shop's own discounts, and doing nothing are also substitutes. No reliable current delivery-platform commission comparison was established, so this report does not invent a Grab/Nham24 fee table. Poket was found as a regional loyalty vendor, but Cambodian deployments were not established and it is not counted as a verified local operator.

## Where an entrant could win — hypotheses

1. **A dense neighborhood network:** rewards people can actually use during coffee, lunch, and dinner routines.
2. **Menu and order integration:** staff complete fewer separate steps than managing a standalone rewards app.
3. **Payment choice:** aim to support verified cash and approved payment-provider flows without requiring a particular bank for membership. This is a product goal, not a claim that universal transaction verification exists today.
4. **Merchant control:** a capped earning budget, a clear item reward catalog, and reliable settlement.
5. **Evidence of value:** show new-to-shop redeemers, repeat purchases, costs, and incremental gross profit where measurement supports it.

A bank can potentially reproduce many features. Defensibility would come from local merchant relationships, workflow usefulness, trusted settlement, and recurring usage—not the word “coin.”

## Bottom-up market sizing exercise

Do not use national QR registrations as TAM. Build a verified list of eligible shops in a reachable district. For each, record location, category, owner access, approximate sales, current POS/rewards, and willingness to participate.

Illustrative funnel, not a forecast: 200 mapped shops → 60 owner conversations → 15 qualified prospects → 5–10 pilot shops. At 100 paying shops and $15/month, annual menu subscription revenue is $18,000 before churn, discounts, taxes, and costs. This deliberately shows the scale needed rather than claiming a huge market.

## Competitor fieldwork before launch

Visit or demo Wingpoints, Sathapana Rewards, ABA Cashback, and Point hub. Record: nearby active outlets, eligibility, earning steps, reward wait time, redemption failures, merchant funding/fees, payout timing, customer ownership/export, cancellation, integration access, and total annual cost. Ask merchants which alternatives they actually use and why. No fieldwork or sales contact has occurred yet.
