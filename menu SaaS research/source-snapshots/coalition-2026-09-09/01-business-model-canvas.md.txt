# Business model canvas

Version 1 · 9 September 2026 · Proposed business design

**Customer promise:** Earn rewards on everyday purchases at participating local shops and use them across the neighborhood.

**Merchant promise:** Run a branded digital menu and measure whether a shared rewards network brings profitable repeat and new visits, with a capped reward budget and clear settlement.

## The nine blocks

| Block | Proposed model |
|---|---|
| **1. Customer segments** | Paying customers: independent cafés, tea shops, bakeries, and small restaurants able to record sales and fund rewards. Users: nearby residents, workers, and students who visit several participating shops. Later customers: small chains and campaign sponsors. Begin with one dense area, not every Cambodian merchant. |
| **2. Value propositions** | Shops: branded menus, staff order workflow, optional funded rewards, redemption reimbursement, and understandable results. Customers: one rewards identity, accessible item rewards, clear balances, and nearby places to redeem. Platform must demonstrate usefulness beyond existing bank rewards. |
| **3. Channels** | Founder-led shop visits, merchant referrals, menu/table QR codes, cashier explanation, merchant social pages, and existing Telegram entry points. Test browser access for people who do not want Telegram. Paid advertising only after activation and retention are measured. |
| **4. Customer relationships** | Assisted merchant onboarding, short Khmer/English training, weekly pilot reviews, monthly statements, and a clear support channel. Customers receive self-service history, claim receipts, balance explanations, and opt-in reminders. |
| **5. Revenue streams** | Menu SaaS subscription; disclosed fee on merchant-funded coin issuance; later optional sponsored campaigns and agreed commissions on purchased-coin redemptions. No separate mandatory loyalty subscription in the proposed initial offer. No reliance on expired points, customer float, or sale of identifiable customer data. |
| **6. Key resources** | Reliable software, tenant isolation, shared rewards ledger, funded redemption obligations, merchant agreements, trusted payment partner, local onboarding/support, and a dense set of useful participating shops. |
| **7. Key activities** | Onboard shops, maintain menus, confirm purchases, issue funded coins, validate redemptions, reconcile and settle, resolve refunds, prevent abuse, measure merchant outcomes, and manage compliance. |
| **8. Key partnerships** | Participating merchants; appropriately licensed bank/payment provider for relevant funds flows; Cambodian lawyer and accountant; optional POS integrators; later prize or campaign sponsors. None are signed partners yet. |
| **9. Cost structure** | Development and maintenance, hosting, customer verification/messaging, merchant acquisition, onboarding/support, payment and payout charges, fraud/refunds, legal/accounting, and funded promotions. Reward principal is tracked separately from spendable operating money. |

## The Nectar inspiration

Nectar's 14 October 2021 investor presentation describes partners paying for issued points, partners receiving agreed payments on redemption, and additional service income. This supports the structure, not the proposed Cambodian rates or profitability. [Sainsbury's presentation, pages 4–5](https://corporate.sainsburys.co.uk/media/yafhro11/j-sainsbury-plc-nectar-deep-dive-event-transcript.pdf)

Our adaptation adds menu SaaS as the initial merchant product. Gold, consumer top-ups, and phone prizes are founder-requested extensions, not features established by that Nectar presentation. Develop an original brand and agreements rather than copying Nectar's name or customer terms.

## Candidate offer to test

- Digital menu subscription: **$15 per shop per month**, an assumption, with clear branch and feature limits defined in the pilot agreement.
- Loyalty enrollment: no separate subscription.
- Reward funding: **$1.10 for each $1 of redemption funding**, giving a 10% markup on reward value, not a 10% commission on food sales.
- Example earn rate: rewards worth **2% of eligible spending**, making the shop's reward-plus-fee cost **2.2% of eligible spending**.
- Merchants set their own item reward prices and availability within the agreed program rules; the platform sets the reimbursement rate.

These are interview prices, not an approved tariff. Report 03 compares this offer with local competitive pricing and shows the limited spread.

## Why each side might participate

A shop funds rewards to encourage future visits and customer acquisition. A redeeming shop receives the agreed payment and may gain a customer or additional paid items. A customer gets useful rewards across several places. The operator receives recurring software revenue and a fee for running the network.

These motivations are hypotheses. Shop A may worry about funding Shop B's customers. The answer must be measured reciprocal visits and profitable outcomes, not a claim that all merchants always benefit equally.

## Positioning to validate

“Your shop's digital menu, plus shared neighborhood rewards with clear costs and weekly reimbursement.”

Avoid claiming “Cambodia's first,” “guaranteed more sales,” or “free rewards for merchants.” Merchant enrollment can be free while reward funding still costs money. Make every fee visible together.
